The watch app is a full tuner with its own microphone — no phone needed, though your instruments and settings arrive from one over [iCloud sync](/guide/#icloud-sync). Its whole design is **tuning without looking**: the wrist taps out how far off you are, and a glance answers anything the taps can't.

## The front door

{{< figure src="/img/guide/root-watch.png" alt="The watch root: starred instruments, All instruments, Chromatic, Settings" class="shot watch" >}}

Your **starred** instruments lead; everything else lives under *All instruments*, grouped by family. Swipe any instrument row to star or unstar it — stars sync, so one given here shines on the phone's launch screen too.

## Tuning a string

{{< figure src="/img/guide/reading-watch.png" alt="The tuner pane reading A4 two cents sharp" class="shot watch" >}}

The pane is pinned to **one string**: its name sits large among its neighbours, the arc sweeps right for sharp and left for flat, the cents sit inside the arc's hollow, and the dot strip repeats the story. When the string has held in tune a moment, its name turns **green** — done — and the screen advances to the next string almost immediately once you play it.

{{< figure src="/img/guide/listening-watch.png" alt="The pane waiting, dimmed, showing Play a note" class="shot watch" >}}

**"Play a note"** with a dimmed arc means it's listening. Nothing moves or reshuffles between notes — a rest just dims the screen.

Switching strings: **turn the crown** or **swipe sideways** for an explicit pick, or simply *play another string on purpose* — a sustained note on a rival string takes the screen, while a brush or a sympathetic ring never does.

## The haptics

The wrist taps at the string's **physical beat rate** against its target: faster means further off, slowing means you're closing in, **silence means in tune** — and a firm double-buzz marks the string settling. The taps carry distance only; the arc gives direction at a glance. An A4 ten cents flat taps about 2½ times a second, and the rate is real physics: it's the beating you'd hear against a reference tone.

## Double stops

{{< figure src="/img/guide/pair-watch.png" alt="Pair mode: two names lit, the beat rate in the readout" class="shot watch" >}}

Bow two adjacent strings and the pane goes pair-shaped: both names light, the arc shows the **interval's** error (upright and green when the pair sits at its targets), the readout shows the beat rate the taps are tapping, and the dot strip splits into two thin rows — the higher string on top. The taps become the pair's own beats, slowing and stopping exactly as the audible ones do. Lift the bow and it's a one-string pane again.

## Intonation, hands-free

Play the focused string open, then its octave — the 12th fret, or the harmonic. Each sample confirms with a **quick double tap** as it locks, and while the octave sounds the readout swaps to the verdict: **Δ +3.1**, green when the saddle lands. Both hands stay on the tools; switching strings starts a fresh measurement. No mode, nothing to turn on.

## The instrument's own screen

The button at the foot of the pane names the tuning you're on — your preset's name, *Standard*, or *Custom* — with the reference and temperament beneath. Tap it for the instrument's details: the lock, the reference stepper, the temperament, and its presets — *Standard* always first, so loading a preset is never a one-way trip.
